Hi there, welcome to my website! I'm currently a graduate student pursuing a PhD in Environmental Science and Engineering at Caltech working with [Tapio Schneider](https://climate-dynamics.org/people/tapio-schneider/) and his research group. I am in my fourth year and expecting to graduate in May 2027.

Prior to Caltech, I got my Bachelor's degree in computer science from Princeton University in 2022, where I worked with [Gabe Vecchi](https://vecchi.princeton.edu/people/gabriel-vecchi) and [Ching-Yao Lai](https://icyphysics.stanford.edu/bio) on various hurricane and machine learning-related projects. 

Generally, I'm interested in anything related to atmospheric science, tropical dynamics, climate modeling, and machine learning. My current research involves large-scale tropical dynamics, including the Hadley cell, monsoons, and midlatitude eddies. The goals of my thesis are to better understand the dynamics of these circulation features and how they all interact to set tropical climate, weather, and seasonality. 

To learn more about my research projects and interest, check out the [research](https://ryaneusebi.github.io/research/) page to learn more, or check out my list of [publications](https://ryaneusebi.github.io/publications/) for links to my papers!

News
------
Check out my latest pre-print about axisymmetric Hadley circulations [here](https://essopenarchive.org/users/800146/articles/1380732-factors-controlling-axisymmetric-hadley-circulations-and-their-seasonality)! Many climate scientists learn in graduate school about the simple semi-analytical model for axisymmetric Hadley circulations, introduced by Held and Hou (1980) and extended to the seasonal cycle by Lindzen and Hou (1988). In our pre-print, we show how and why this model is inappropriate for the seasonal cycle, and we introduce a new-semi analytical model that is much more accurate. The solution has strong implications for monsoons and the seasonal cycle of Earth's tropical circulation, which we discuss throughout the paper!
